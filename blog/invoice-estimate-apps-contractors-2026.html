Best Invoice and Estimate Apps for Contractors in 2026: What to Actually Look For

By A&J Tech · Published October 8, 2026

If you're a contractor, your invoicing app isn't office software. It's a jobsite tool. It lives in your pocket, gets opened with dusty hands between the truck and the front door, and earns its keep in the two minutes between "how much will this cost?" and "sounds good, let's do it."

This guide compares five real options — including one we build ourselves (FieldBuddy, which we'll disclose throughout rather than pretend we don't make it). Every app below is real, with real pricing taken from public sources as of September 2026. Ratings and install figures cited are what the apps publicly show on Google Play.

What actually matters in an invoice and estimate app for contractors

Forget feature-count bingo. The things that decide whether you'll still use the app in three months:

  1. Speed on-site. You should be able to build an estimate standing in a customer's driveway. If it takes longer than writing it on the back of a work order, you'll stop using it.
  2. The estimate-to-invoice flow. The single most valuable workflow in this category: write the estimate once, then convert it to an invoice in one tap when the job's done. Every app here does some version of this, but the smoothness varies.
  3. Pricing you can predict. Contractors get burned by per-user seats, volume caps ("5 invoices a month, then upgrade"), and feature ladders that bury the essentials on the top tier. Read the pricing section below carefully — it's where most of these apps differ.
  4. Offline capability. Basements, rural jobsites, and metal buildings eat cell signal. An app that needs connectivity to open a template isn't a jobsite tool.
  5. Deposit and payment handling. Deposits are how contractors survive. The apps that understand trades — deposits on estimates, milestone schedules, change orders — beat generic invoicing apps every time.

Pricing models compared

Pricing in this category falls into four camps:

  • Flat monthly, contractor-specific: GetCost (from ~$9.99/mo), Oficio Pro ($19.99/mo or $149.99/yr ≈ $12.50/mo), Joist Pro ($17/mo). Joist adds tiers up to Run at $70/mo for bigger operations.
  • Per-user, suite-style: Contractor+ Pro is ~$14/user/mo on annual billing — cheap for a solo operator, expensive once you add crew.
  • Volume-capped general invoicing: Invoice2go's pricing is based on invoices per month, not features. Starter ($5.99/mo) caps you at 2 invoices a month; most working contractors need the Premium tier at $39.99/mo for unlimited.
  • Accounting-first: FreshBooks starts at $23/mo and climbs to $70/mo plus per-user fees — real accounting, but it's the priciest entry point here.
  • Cheapest entry: InvoiceOwl's base tier is $4/mo on annual billing — the lowest price of the bunch.

FieldBuddy's approach (ours): fair flat pricing after a free start, no per-seat tax, no invoice-count caps. We built the pricing the way we'd want it as users.

Joist — Invoice & Estimate App (4.6★, 1M+ installs)

What it is: The category's market leader by adoption. Estimates, invoices, scheduling with a calendar view, job organization with photos and notes, client management, and payment tracking.

Strengths: It accepts credit cards in-app (plus Tap to Pay), offers homeowner financing at checkout, and its higher tiers add change orders, contracts with eSignatures, QuickBooks sync, expense tracking, and automated Google review requests.

Caveats: The tier ladder is aggressive — Basics is $10/mo but caps you at 5 documents a month, and the good stuff (change orders, eSignatures) lives at Pro ($17/mo) and Elite ($32/mo). A $49+/mo AI receptionist add-on also exists.

Best for: Contractors who want one platform from lead to payment and don't mind paying for it.

Contractor+ — the "operating system" play (4.4★, ~110K installs)

What it is: The broadest feature surface in this comparison. Estimates, invoices, a CRM with sales pipeline, built-in business phone with two-way SMS, dispatch and scheduling with Gantt charts, GPS time clock, and real-time QuickBooks Online sync.

Strengths: Its "Estimatic AI" builds quotes from a job description or photo using live material pricing (Lowe's, Menards, ABC Supply) plus local labor rates. Contractor+ Pay handles cards (2.7%) and ACH (1%, capped at $10). There's a genuinely free-forever plan for one user.

Caveats: It's a lot of software. If you just need estimates and invoices, the breadth is overhead — and per-user pricing adds up with a crew.

Best for: Growing businesses that want dispatch, crew management, and CRM in one place.

Invoice2go (by BILL) — 4.6★, 1M+ installs

What it is: A polished general-SMB invoicing app: invoices, estimates, quotes, recurring invoices, expense tracking, a projects hub, and wide integrations (QuickBooks, Xero, Zapier).

Strengths: Automatic payment reminders, client view tracking ("they opened your invoice"), and 160-country multi-language support.

Caveats: Pricing is volume-capped — 2 invoices/month on Starter, 5/month on Professional. Most working contractors land on Premium at $39.99/mo. It's not contractor-specific: no trade features like deposits workflows or change orders at the core.

Best for: Solo contractors or tradespeople who also do non-trade work and want a general invoicing tool.

InvoiceOwl — the budget option (4.5★, ~50K installs)

What it is: A straightforward invoicing app: unlimited invoices, 50+ template colors, estimate→invoice in one tap, credit memos, one-click deposit requests, and view tracking.

Strengths: The cheapest entry point here at $4/mo (annual). Unlimited invoices even at base. Higher tiers add e-signatures, recurring billing, SMS reminders, and automatic follow-up sequences.

Caveats: No QuickBooks sync found. Fewer contractor-native features than Joist or Contractor+ — it's an invoicing app, not a trade platform.

Best for: Cost-conscious contractors who want clean invoices and deposit requests without a big subscription.

The 5-question choosing checklist

  1. How many documents do I actually send per month? If it's more than a handful, avoid volume-capped tiers — you'll outgrow them in a month.
  2. Do I need to take card payments inside the app? If yes, shortlist Joist, Contractor+, or Invoice2go. If you're happy with Venmo/Zelle/bank transfer instructions on the invoice, the field opens up.
  3. Am I solo, or do I have a crew? Per-user pricing punishes growth. Flat pricing rewards it.
  4. Do I want an invoicing app or a business platform? Contractor+ and Joist Elite are platforms (CRM, dispatch, marketing). FieldBuddy and InvoiceOwl are focused tools. Neither is wrong — but buying a platform when you need a tool wastes money, and buying a tool when you need a platform wastes time.
  5. Will it work in a basement? Ask about offline capability before you buy. Test it on a real jobsite, not your office Wi-Fi.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoice and estimate app for contractors?

There's no single best app — it depends on your needs. Joist is the most adopted (1M+ installs) and the most full-featured for contractors. Contractor+ is the best all-in-one platform with dispatch and CRM. InvoiceOwl is the cheapest entry point at $4/mo. FieldBuddy (our app) focuses on fair flat pricing and the core estimate-to-invoice workflow for solo contractors.

How much does a contractor invoicing app cost in 2026?

Options range from $4/mo (InvoiceOwl, annual) to $70/mo (Joist Run) plus per-user fees on some platforms. Contractor-specific sweet spot: roughly $10–$20/mo for a solo operator.

Should contractors use a general invoicing app or a contractor-specific one?

Contractor-specific apps (Joist, Contractor+, FieldBuddy, GetCost) understand deposits, change orders, and job tracking. General apps (Invoice2go, FreshBooks) are stronger on accounting and integrations. Most contractors do better with a trade-specific tool for daily work.
